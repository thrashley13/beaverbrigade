Gold Fever • 12-stop self-guided tour

⛏ Holcomb Valley Gold Rush field guide

The historic tour is about 10.9 miles over dirt roads from the Discovery Center route description, with numbered stops marked by the crossed pick-and-shovel symbol. Use onX for navigation; use this guide to know why you're stopping.

Mileage below is cumulative from the traditional Gold Fever route. Road access remains conditional on the pre-trip Forest Service status check.

1 · Holcomb Valley View Trail — 3.2 mi

What happened: William F. Holcomb came into this country in 1860 while hunting bear. From this overlook you get the broader view of the valley that soon drew prospectors by the hundreds.

Look for: the Holcomb Valley panorama and the transition from mountain forest into the historic mining basin.

2 · Last Chance Placer — 4.8 mi

What happened: This was placer country: miners washed gravel and sand with water to separate heavier gold. The Forest Service guide describes pay dirt being hauled in sacks and worked with water.

Look for: old tailings/sand mounds and disturbed ground from placer workings. The modern marker may be easy to miss.

3 · Two Gun Bill's Saloon — 5.8 mi

Story: Holcomb Valley developed the saloons, gambling and rough frontier culture that followed the gold rush. The interpretive stop commemorates Two Gun Bill's Saloon; historical route researchers caution that the marked interpretive location may not be the exact original saloon site.

Look for: the marked remains/site and imagine the mining-camp settlement that once surrounded it.

4 · Jonathan Tibbets' Grasshopper Quartz Mill — 6.3 mi

What happened: A high piece of ground known as Chinamen's Knoll held a small steam-powered mill that pulverized gold-bearing ore from the John Bull mines.

Look for: old sand/tailings mounds and a rusted water pump—the visible remnants of the milling operation.

5 · Hangman's Tree — 6.5 mi

Story: The tree became a symbol of frontier justice in the boomtown era. Local tradition says condemned men were hanged from its limbs and branches were cut afterward.

Look for: the fenced, heavily altered old tree. Treat the hanging-count folklore as legend rather than a precise historical record.

6 · Original Gold Diggings — 6.8 mi

Why it matters: This is the area associated with Bill Holcomb's original 1860 gold discovery—the event that ignited the Holcomb Valley rush. Contemporary interpretation describes placer claims staked throughout the area soon afterward.

Look for: the meadow/seasonal drainage and signs of old placer disturbance rather than a standing structure.

7 · Belleville — 7.1 mi

What happened: Belleville became the valley's largest settlement, with stores, saloons and dance halls. At the peak of the rush it reportedly had about 1,500 residents and in 1861 narrowly lost a bid to become the San Bernardino County seat by two votes.

Look for: the interpretive log cabin representing the type of building once common here; it is not an original Belleville structure.

8 · Arrastres / Gold Ore Grinders — 7.1 mi

How it worked: Before sophisticated mills, miners crushed quartz in an arrastre: a heavy drag stone was pulled in a circular stone-lined pit, historically by animal power, grinding ore so the gold could be recovered.

Look for: the circular stone remains just beyond the Belleville cabin area. Stay off the historic structure.

9 · Ross' Grave — 7.3 mi

Story: Little is firmly documented about Ross beyond his name and accidental death while cutting a tree. He was buried at the spot. The original grave was later damaged; a corner post and some pickets remained when the interpretive guide was prepared.

Look for: the gravesite reached by a roughly 500-foot walk. Treat it as a burial site.

10 · Pygmy Cabin Site — 7.3 mi

Mystery: A tiny log cabin—only a little over four feet high at the walls—once stood here. Its purpose and builder were uncertain even in the historic guide. It was destroyed by fire in November 1983.

Look for: the former cabin location rather than a surviving building.

11 · Metzger Mine — 9.0 mi

What happened: As easy placer deposits declined, miners pursued gold-bearing quartz veins underground. The Metzger workings followed one such vein; the old horizontal mine entrance/adit remained visible when the guide was produced.

Safety: observe mine features from outside. Never enter an abandoned mine.

12 · Gold Mountain / Lucky Baldwin Mine — 10.8 mi

What happened: One of the area's major later hard-rock operations. Elias J. “Lucky” Baldwin acquired the operation in the 1870s; a large stamp mill and cyanide processing plant followed, and mining continued into the early 20th century.

Look for: mining ruins and the Gold Mountain setting above Baldwin Lake. The Forest Service guide notes the old wagon road connection back toward Holcomb Valley.

Why this matters: the stops tell a progression: discovery and placer mining → boomtown life → ore processing → hard-rock quartz mining. That gives us a coherent story as we drive instead of twelve unexplained markers.

📻 Beaver Brigade Radio

PRIMARY: CH 16 · CODE 1

CTCSS 67.0 Hz · direct mode · narrow bandwidth

BACKUP: CH 17 · CODE 1

CTCSS 67.0 Hz · switch only when lead calls the group over.

Buc-ee's pre-roll radio check

  1. Everyone on 16 / Code 1.
  2. Lead calls each rig; each driver replies.
  3. Everyone switches together to 17 / Code 1.
  4. Repeat two-way check.
  5. Everyone returns to 16 / Code 1.
  6. Do not roll until all four rigs pass.

Radio problem?

Verify channel → verify CTCSS/privacy code → use Monitor/turn receive filtering off → verify narrow bandwidth → check antenna upright → retest both directions.
